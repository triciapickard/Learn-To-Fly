## Why a special language

Radio is noisy, and many pilots share one frequency. Aviation uses a standard way of speaking so that every word is clear the first time, even through static. It's also short, so the frequency stays free for others.

## The phonetic alphabet

Letters are spoken as words, so "B" and "D" can't be confused:

| Letter | Word    | Letter | Word     | Letter | Word    |
| ------ | ------- | ------ | -------- | ------ | ------- |
| A      | Alfa    | J      | Juliett  | S      | Sierra  |
| B      | Bravo   | K      | Kilo     | T      | Tango   |
| C      | Charlie | L      | Lima     | U      | Uniform |
| D      | Delta   | M      | Mike     | V      | Victor  |
| E      | Echo    | N      | November | W      | Whiskey |
| F      | Foxtrot | O      | Oscar    | X      | X-ray   |
| G      | Golf    | P      | Papa     | Y      | Yankee  |
| H      | Hotel   | Q      | Quebec   | Z      | Zulu    |
| I      | India   | R      | Romeo    |        |         |

## Numbers

Most numbers are said normally, with a few exceptions to make them clearer:

- **3** is "tree", **5** is "fife" and **9** is "niner".
- Say each digit separately: 123 is "one two three".
- Altitudes use thousands and hundreds: 3,500 is "three thousand five hundred".
- Headings are always three digits: 090 is "heading zero niner zero".
- Frequencies: 122.8 is "one two two point eight".

## Call signs

In the US, a civil airplane's call sign is its registration, an **N-number** such as N172SP. On the radio you can:

- Say it in full: "November One Seven Two Sierra Papa".
- Replace "November" with the airplane type: "**Skyhawk** One Seven Two Sierra Papa".
- After the controller has shortened it, use the short form too: "Skyhawk Two Sierra Papa".

In this course we use **Skyhawk 123** ("Skyhawk One Two Three") in the examples.

## Putting a call together

A first call to someone has four parts:

1. **Who you're calling**: "Livermore Ground"
2. **Who you are**: "Skyhawk One Two Three"
3. **Where you are**: "at the south ramp"
4. **What you want**: "request taxi for departure, with information Alfa"

> "Livermore Ground, Skyhawk One Two Three, at the south ramp, request taxi for departure with information Alfa."

## Good radio habits

- **Listen before you talk.** Make sure nobody else is mid-conversation.
- **Think first, then press the button.** Know what you're going to say.
- **Keep it short.** Leave out "um", "please" and "over".
- **Read back** the important parts of instructions. Lesson 7.3 covers which.

## Radios on the G1000

The Skyhawk has two communication radios, **COM1** and **COM2**. Each has an **active** frequency, the one you're using, and a **standby** frequency, the one you're setting up next.

1. Tune the standby frequency with the **COM** knob.
2. Press the **flip-flop** (double-arrow) key to swap standby and active.

The G1000 shows the frequencies at the top right of both screens.

## ATIS

At larger airports, the **ATIS** (Automatic Terminal Information Service) is a recording of the current weather, runway in use and other notes. Each new recording gets a letter: "information Alfa", then "Bravo", and so on.

Listen to it before you call, and tell the controller which one you have: "with information Alfa". It saves the controller from repeating it all.

## Homework: listen

Spend 15 minutes on **LiveATC**, listening to a towered airport near you or to Livermore. You'll start to hear the patterns: short calls, standard phrases and read-backs.

:::callout{type="sim"}

:::
