## Where this course takes you

By the end of Learn to Fly you will fly the Cessna 172 Skyhawk in Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024 the way a student pilot is taught to fly the real one. You will take off, fly a traffic pattern and land on the centerline. You will handle a stall and an engine failure calmly. And you will plan and fly a short cross-country flight with a chart, a VOR and the G1000.

Each lesson explains why the airplane behaves the way it does, so the skills carry over to any airplane you fly next.

## How the course is organized

The course has nine **modules**, in the same order a flight school teaches:

| Module | Topic                               |
| ------ | ----------------------------------- |
| 0      | Getting started                     |
| 1      | Meet the Skyhawk                    |
| 2      | Fundamentals of flight              |
| 3      | Ground operations                   |
| 4      | Takeoffs, patterns and landings     |
| 5      | Slow flight, stalls and emergencies |
| 6      | VFR navigation                      |
| 7      | Radio and airport operations        |
| 8      | Capstone                            |

Each module follows the same loop:

1. **Learn**: a short lesson explains one idea at a time.
2. **See**: an interactive diagram lets you play with it.
3. **Try**: quick questions check that it clicked.
4. **Fly**: a challenge puts it to work in the sim.
5. **Reflect**: a debrief helps you see what to do better next time.

## How a challenge works

Every challenge has three steps: **Brief, Fly, Debrief**.

- **Brief.** The brief gives the exact sim setup: aircraft, airport, weather, time and fuel. Everyone flies the same conditions, so results are comparable. Read it with the sim paused.
- **Fly.** Keep the Fly tab open on a second screen, a tablet or a phone. It shows the steps, the key numbers and an optional timer.
- **Debrief.** After landing, score yourself against the rubric. Each criterion has a clear, measurable description, for example "altitude within ±100 ft".

Your score earns a tier: **Bronze** means you passed, **Silver** means you flew it well, and **Gold** means you flew it to a high standard. Scores are self-assessed, so they only help if you mark what you actually flew. If you missed a criterion, fly the challenge again.

## What you need

- **Microsoft Flight Simulator 2024**, any edition. The course uses the Cessna 172 Skyhawk with the G1000 NXi glass cockpit.
- **A controller.** A gamepad works. A joystick with a twist grip, or a yoke with rudder pedals, is better. Lesson 0.2 sets up all three.
- **Ideally a second screen**, a tablet or a phone for the brief and the Fly tab, so you can read them without leaving the cockpit.

## Learn to Fly is in public beta

The site is new. Every module that is open has been flown and checked in MSFS 2024, but you may still find a typo, a rough edge or a bug, and some pages will change as feedback comes in. Modules that are still being written stay closed until they are verified. If something looks wrong, or you have an idea, open an issue on [GitHub](https://github.com/triciapickard/Learn-To-Fly/issues). Every report is read.

## Sim training is not flight training

:::callout{type="safety"}
Learn to Fly is for simulation only. It is not flight instruction, it does not count toward any pilot certificate, and it must never be used for real-world flight training or navigation. If you want to fly a real airplane, find a certificated flight instructor.
:::

A simulator is a good way to learn procedures, build a mental picture and practice decision making. It cannot teach you how a real airplane feels, how to handle real weather, or how to stay safe with real risks. Many real pilots use sims to stay sharp, and many real students find the sim makes their lessons easier. Treat the sim as a warm-up for real flying.

## Tips for success

- **Keep sessions short.** Twenty to forty minutes of focused practice beats three tired hours.
- **Fly each challenge more than once.** The second attempt is where you learn the most.
- **Use pause.** Pausing to look around or read the brief is part of how you learn.
- **Celebrate good decisions.** A go-around when things don't look right is a good decision.
