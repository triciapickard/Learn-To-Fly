## What to expect

This first flight is about **feel**. There's no score to worry about: the challenge [[c0-1-first-flight-over-livermore]] passes as long as you take off and make a few turns. You'll learn the proper technique for everything you do today in later modules.

Expect the airplane to feel twitchy at first. That's normal. Everyone over-controls on their first flight.

:::callout{type="tip"}
Small inputs. Then wait. Then small inputs again. The airplane needs a moment to respond to each movement you make.
:::

## Five things to watch

1. **The horizon.** Where it sits in the windshield tells you whether the nose is up, down or level, and whether the wings are level.
2. **Airspeed.** Shown on the tape on the left of the PFD, in knots.
3. **Altitude.** Shown on the tape on the right, in feet above sea level.
4. **Heading.** The compass at the bottom of the PFD shows which way the nose points.
5. **The runway.** On the way out and on the way back, know where it is.

Look outside most of the time. Glance at the instruments, then look back outside.

## Step by step

Start on runway 25R at Livermore with the engine running. Pause the sim and read these steps first.

1. **Unpause and add full power smoothly.** The airplane starts to roll. Keep the nose pointed down the centerline with small rudder inputs (or let auto-rudder help).
2. **At {{vspeed.vr}}, gently raise the nose.** The airplane lifts off. Hold the nose a little above the horizon and let it climb at about {{vspeed.vy}}.
3. **Climb to 3,000 ft MSL.** Then lower the nose to the level-flight picture and reduce power to about 2,300 RPM. The airspeed settles around 100 knots.
4. **Make gentle turns.** Roll to about 15–20° of bank, hold it, then roll level. Try a turn left toward Lake Del Valle, south of the airport, and a turn right toward Mt Diablo, the big mountain to the north.
5. **Look around.** Find Interstate 580 running east–west through the valley, the town of Livermore, and the wind turbines on the Altamont Pass hills to the east.

:::callout{type="verify"}
Author note: fly this route in MSFS 2024 and check the runway designation, the landmarks' positions and the level-flight speed at 2,300 RPM.
:::

## Coming back

When you're done, you have two choices:

- **End the flight** from the pause menu. Perfectly fine for today.
- **Try to land.** Fly back toward the airport, line up with a runway from a long way out, reduce power and descend gently. Don't worry if it isn't pretty: landing is what Module 4 is for.

If things start to feel like too much at any point, **pause**. Take a breath, look at where you are, read the next step, then unpause.

:::quiz{id="l0-3-q2" type="single"}
What should you do if you feel overwhelmed during a flight?

- [ ] Keep going and hope it gets better
- [x] Pause the sim
- [ ] Turn on the autopilot
- [ ] Restart the flight immediately

---

Pause. It freezes everything so you can look around, re-read the brief and make a plan. Real pilots can't pause, which is exactly why the sim is such a good place to learn.
:::

## Reflect

After the flight, think about these questions. The debrief for [[c0-1-first-flight-over-livermore]] asks them too:

- What surprised you?
- What felt hardest?
- On a scale of 1 to 5, how confident do you feel?

Write down your answers. When you finish Module 4, fly this challenge again and compare.
