## Decisions, not just skills

Most accidents don't happen because a pilot couldn't fly the airplane. They happen because of a decision: taking off into weather that was too bad, pressing on when fuel was low, or skipping a check to save time.

**Aeronautical decision making** (ADM) is the FAA's name for thinking through risks in a structured way, before and during a flight.

## Hazardous attitudes

The FAA lists five attitudes that lead to bad decisions, each with an antidote:

| Attitude        | Sounds like                    | Antidote                                   |
| --------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| Anti-authority  | "The rules don't apply to me." | Follow the rules. They're usually right.   |
| Impulsivity     | "Do something, quickly!"       | Not so fast. Think first.                  |
| Invulnerability | "It won't happen to me."       | It could happen to me.                     |
| Macho           | "I can do it."                 | Taking chances is foolish.                 |
| Resignation     | "What's the use?"              | I'm not helpless. I can make a difference. |

## PAVE: before the flight

Use **PAVE** to check the four areas of risk:

- **P — Pilot.** Are you current, rested and ready? Use IMSAFE, below.
- **A — Aircraft.** Is it airworthy, fueled and right for this flight?
- **V — enVironment.** Weather, terrain, airspace, runway length, daylight.
- **E — External pressures.** Anything pushing you to go: an appointment, passengers, wanting to get home.

External pressures are the sneakiest. Make a plan B before you go, so you never feel you _have_ to continue.

## IMSAFE: the pilot

Check yourself before every flight:

- **I**llness. Do you feel unwell?
- **M**edication. Are you taking anything that affects you?
- **S**tress. Is something weighing on your mind?
- **A**lcohol. The rules set a minimum of 8 hours "bottle to throttle", and many pilots use 24.
- **F**atigue. Did you sleep well?
- **E**motion and **E**ating. Are you upset? Have you eaten and had water?

## Personal minimums

The regulations set the legal minimums. **Personal minimums** are your own, stricter limits, based on your experience. Write them down **before** you need them, when you're not under pressure.

A starting point for a new pilot: wind no more than 15 kt, crosswind no more than 8 kt, visibility at least 5 sm, cloud base at least 3,000 ft, and at least 1 hour of fuel at landing. As your skills grow, you can relax them, one at a time.

## Why it matters in a sim

Nobody gets hurt in a sim. But the habits you build here are the ones you'll take into a real airplane. Run PAVE before each challenge, and treat a sim go-around or diversion as a good decision, not a failure.
