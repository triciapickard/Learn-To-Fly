## Where to do the run-up

Stop in the **run-up area** near the end of the runway:

- **Into the wind** if you can, which helps engine cooling.
- **Clear of other airplanes**, so your propeller blast doesn't hit them.
- **Parking brake set**, because you'll run the engine at high power.

## The engine run-up

With the parking brake set and your feet on the brakes:

1. **Throttle to about 1,800 RPM.**
2. **Magneto check.** Turn the key from BOTH to **L**: RPM drops slightly. Back to **BOTH**. Then **R**, and back to **BOTH**. A small drop on each is normal; a large drop, or a big difference between the two, means a problem.
3. **Engine instruments.** Oil pressure and temperature, and the electrical system, all in the green.
4. **Throttle to idle.** Check the engine keeps running smoothly, then set about 1,000 RPM.

:::callout{type="verify"}
Author note: check the run-up RPM and magneto drop limits against the MSFS 2024 in-sim checklist (commonly 1,800 RPM, a drop of no more than 150 RPM on each magneto, and no more than 50 RPM difference between them).
:::

:::callout{type="safety"}
Always finish the magneto check with the key back on BOTH. Taking off on one magneto costs power and removes your backup.
:::

## Controls free and correct

Move the yoke fully left, right, forward and back, and the rudder pedals both ways. Look out at each surface (or switch to an outside view in the sim) and check it moves **freely** and in the **correct** direction. Yoke left: left aileron up.

## Before-takeoff items

- **Flaps** set for takeoff (0–10°).
- **Trim** set to the takeoff position.
- **Fuel selector** on BOTH.
- **Mixture** rich (at Bay Area airports).
- **Transponder** on ALT.
- **Lights** on: strobes and landing light.
- **Doors** closed and latched.

Run the before-takeoff checklist below.

::checklist{slug="before-takeoff"}

## The takeoff briefing

Say it out loud, every time. Deciding what you'll do **before** something goes wrong turns a scary surprise into a plan. For example:

> "This will be a normal takeoff from runway 25R. Rotate at 55, climb at 74. If the engine fails on the runway, I'll close the throttle and brake. If it fails after takeoff with runway left, I'll land straight ahead. If it fails with no runway left, I'll lower the nose to glide at 68 and land in the best spot within 30° of straight ahead. No turning back below 1,000 ft."

:::quiz{id="l3-3-q1" type="single"}
Your engine fails at 300 ft after takeoff, with no runway left. What does your takeoff briefing say to do?

- [ ] Turn back to the runway
- [x] Lower the nose to best glide and land more or less straight ahead
- [ ] Keep the nose up and try to restart the engine
- [ ] Climb to 1,000 ft first

---

**Lower the nose to best glide ({{vspeed.vg}}) and land more or less straight ahead.** Turning back at low altitude is one of the most common ways pilots lose control. You'll practice this in Module 5.
:::

## After landing

Once you've landed and slowed to taxi speed:

1. **Clear the runway.** Turn off at a taxiway and keep going until the **whole airplane** is past the hold short line. Then stop.
2. **Flaps up.**
3. **Transponder** to standby (or as the checklist says).
4. **Lights** as needed: strobes and landing light off, taxi light on.

::checklist{slug="after-landing"}

:::quiz{id="l3-3-q2" type="single"}
After landing, when is it safe to stop on the taxiway?

- [ ] As soon as the nose wheel reaches the taxiway
- [ ] As soon as you're off the runway pavement
- [x] Once the whole airplane is past the hold short line
- [ ] Only when you reach the ramp

---

Stop only once the **whole airplane** is past the hold short line (on the solid side). Until then, you're still in the runway's protected area.
:::

## Shutdown

At parking:

1. **Parking brake** set.
2. **Avionics master** off.
3. **Mixture** to idle cut-off. The engine stops.
4. **Magnetos** off, key out.
5. **Master switch** off.
